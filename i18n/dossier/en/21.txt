======================================================================
19. GLOSSARY OF NOTIONS AND EXAM INSTRUCTIONS
======================================================================

Fuller definitions, assumptions and calculations are in the chapters.
This glossary lets you find an English word without knowing its chapter.

19.1 Foundations and markets
Absolute advantage — absolute advantage : producing with fewer resources.
Accounting cost/profit — accounting cost/profit : explicit spending/balance.
Allocation — allocation : who receives which goods/resources.
Barrier to entry — barrier to entry : an obstacle to a new competitor.
Binding constraint — binding constraint : actually limits the choice.
Ceteris paribus — all else equal : isolate one change.
Commodity — a standardised basic product, useful for the competitive model.
Competitive reallocation — reallocation towards more productive firms.
Comparative advantage — comparative advantage : lower relative opportunity cost.
Complementary goods — complements : the price of one lowers demand for the other.
Consumer — consumer : chooses a bundle under constraints.
Demand curve/function — demand curve/function : desired purchases by price.
Differentiated products — differentiated products : distinct characteristics.
Distribution — distribution of income/gains; to be distinguished from efficiency.
Economic cost/profit — economic cost/profit : includes forgone opportunities.
Economic rent — economic rent : income linked to a scarce resource.
Efficiency — efficiency : an explicit gains/resources objective, not mere equality.
Equilibrium — equilibrium : consistent decisions/quantities, not necessarily a social optimum.
Excess demand / shortage — excess demand/shortage : QD>QS at the current price.
Excess supply / surplus — excess supply : QS>QD; "surplus" does not
  always mean welfare surplus.
Exit / entry — firms leaving/entering a market.
Free entry — free entry : access with no barrier preventing LR adjustment.
Homogeneous product — homogeneous product : buyers treat it as identical.
Input — input/factor : a resource used to produce.
Inverse demand/supply — inverse demand/supply : price written as a function of Q.
Market clearing price — equilibrium price : equalises QD and QS.
Market definition — market definition : products, geography, period.
Model — a simplified representation with consistent assumptions and implications.
Opportunity cost — opportunity cost : the best alternative given up.
Output — the production obtained, distinct from the inputs used.
Partial equilibrium — partial equilibrium : analysing one market, isolating the rest.
Price taker — price taker : believes its individual choice does not affect p.
Scarcity — scarcity : limited resources force choices/trade-offs.
Shift — a shift of a curve; movement along — a movement along a curve.
Substitute goods — substitutes : the price of one raises demand for the other.
Supply curve/function — supply : quantity desired at each price.
Zero economic profit — zero economic profit : opportunity costs are covered.

19.2 Consumer and behaviour
Affordable/feasible bundle — affordable bundle : respects the budget/constraints.
Altruism — altruism : utility rises with others' well-being/gain.
Behavioral economics — behavioural economics : enriches simple rational choice.
Budget constraint/line — budget constraint/line : possible spending.
Bundle/basket — bundle : a combination of quantities of goods.
Cardinal utility — cardinal utility : interpretable levels/intensities;
  does not match the standard ordinal use of the course's functions.
Cognitive limitations — cognitive limits : imperfect optimisation/information.
Complete preferences — complete preferences : any pair can be compared.
Compensation — a transfer restoring the possibility of a bundle or a utility level,
  depending on the definition used.
Convex preferences — convex preferences : a taste for mixtures, in the usual form.
Corner solution — corner solution : one of the goods chosen at quantity 0.
Diminishing marginal utility — marginal utility falling with quantity.
Engel curve — Engel curve : quantity as a function of income, prices fixed.
Envy / spite — envy / spite : another's gain can lower one's own utility.
Framing — framing effect : presentation affecting the decision.
Heuristic — heuristic : a simple rule used to decide.
Homo economicus — a stylised rational agent; its preferences are not
  necessarily exclusively materialistic.
Income effect — income effect : change due to real purchasing power.
Indifference curve — indifference curve : the same utility for all its bundles.
Inferior good — inferior good : demand falls when income rises.
Interior solution — interior solution : strictly positive goods/inputs.
Loss aversion — loss aversion : weight of losses relative to gains.
Lump-sum payment — lump-sum transfer : an amount independent of the marginal purchase.
Luxury good — luxury good : demand rises more than proportionally with income.
Marginal utility — marginal utility : contribution of the extra unit.
More-is-better / non-satiation — non-satiation : more of desired goods is preferred.
Normal good — normal good : demand rises with income.
Ordinal utility — ordinal utility : used to rank, not to measure absolute happiness.
Overconfidence — overconfidence : overestimating precision/abilities.
Perfect complements — perfect complements : consumption in fixed proportions.
Perfect substitutes — perfect substitutes : exchange at a constant rate.
Preference — preference : a ranking of alternatives.
Self-control — self-control : trade-off between immediate gratification/future goal.
Social preferences — social preferences : utility also depends on others/norms.
Substitution effect — substitution effect : response to the relative price.
Transitivity — transitivity : consistent ranking across several bundles.
Ultimatum game — ultimatum game : a proposed split, accepted or rejected.
Utility maximization — utility maximisation : the best affordable bundle.

19.3 Production and costs
Average product — average product : output per unit of input.
Capital-labor ratio — capital/labour ratio : K/L.
Constant economies of scale — constant LR average cost over the relevant range.
Cost function — minimum cost function given output and input prices.
Cost minimization — cost minimisation for an imposed output.
Decreasing returns to scale — all inputs multiplied by t, output by less than t.
Diminishing marginal product — falling marginal product of a single input.
Diseconomies of scale — diseconomies : rising average cost.
Economies of scale — economies : falling average cost.
Factor of production — factor of production : labour, capital, land, other inputs.
Fixed cost — a cost independent of Q over the horizon.
Increasing returns to scale — all inputs multiplied by t, output by more than t.
Intermediate input — intermediate input : a produced good used to produce.
Isocost — isocost : input combinations with the same cost.
Isoquant — isoquant : input combinations with the same output.
Labor — labour, measured in people/hours depending on the question.
Learning-by-doing — learning by doing, which can lower costs.
Long run — all factors can be adjusted.
Marginal cost — the cost of additional output.
Marginal product — extra output from an extra input.
Production function — the technology/inputs/output relation.
Productivity — output obtained for the resources used.
Raw material — raw material used in production.
Returns to scale — response of Q to a proportional change in all inputs.
Short run — at least one factor is fixed.
Shutdown — short-run halt of production; different from definitive/LR exit.
Sunk cost — unrecoverable cost; must not distort a forward-looking comparison.
Variable cost — a cost varying with output.

19.4 Market power and pricing strategy
Arbitrage / resale — buying/reselling to profit from a price gap.
Best response / reaction function — the optimal choice conditional on the rival.
Bertrand competition — competition in prices.
Bundling — selling in bundles; pure=bundle only, mixed=bundle and separate items.
Capacity constraint — a limit on possible output/seats/sales.
Collusion — coordination between competitors; different from non-cooperative Nash.
Cournot competition — competition in simultaneous quantities.
Duopoly — oligopoly with two firms.
Excess capacity — excess capacity in the sense of output below minimum ATC.
First-degree price discrimination — pricing at each individual marginal WTP.
First-mover advantage — the first player's advantage, depends on the game.
Freemium — a free version plus paid options, self-selection/network.
Horizontal differentiation — diverse tastes, no universal quality ranking.
Incumbent — a firm already present before entry.
Integrated market — an unsegmented market, arbitrage or a common price.
Lerner index — the price/marginal-cost markup divided by price.
Market power — the ability to set a price above marginal cost.
Market share — share of the market, q_i/Q; not a synonym for profit.
Markup — the price-cost margin; specify absolute, relative to price or to cost.
Monopolistic competition — many differentiated firms with easy entry.
Monopoly — a single seller with no close substitutes in the relevant market.
Natural monopoly — a cost technology favouring a single producer.
Nash equilibrium — no profitable unilateral deviation.
Oligopoly — a small number of firms with strategic interaction.
Parallel imports — parallel imports preventing price segmentation.
Patent — a temporary right of exclusion, an incentive to innovate in the model.
Perfect competition — price takers, homogeneity, many agents, free entry.
Price discrimination — different prices across units/customers beyond cost alone.
Profit maximization — the best feasible profit, not the largest quantity.
Reservation price — reservation price, maximum WTP for a unit/transaction.
Second-degree discrimination — self-selection within a menu.
Segmented markets — groups between which resale/transfer is limited.
Self-selection — the customer reveals their type through the offer chosen.
Single-price monopoly — a monopoly charging the same price for every unit.
Stackelberg leadership — sequential order, the follower's reaction is observed.
Strategic complements — best response rises with the rival's choice, e.g. prices.
Strategic substitutes — best response falls with the rival's choice, e.g. Cournot Q.
Third-degree discrimination — observable groups with different prices.
Two-part tariff — an access fee plus a usage price.
Vertical differentiation — rankable quality, consumers differ in WTP.

19.5 Welfare, environment and information
Adverse selection — adverse selection linked to a private type.
Asymmetric information — information that differs between parties.
Bargaining cost / transaction cost — the cost of negotiating/enforcing an agreement.
Certification — quality attested by a third party or a standard.
Club good — excludable, non-rival absent congestion.
Coase theorem — efficiency through costless bargaining with well-defined rights.
Common resource — rival, non-excludable under the rules considered.
Consumer/producer surplus — monetary gains of buyers/sellers beyond
  payment/variable cost.
Deadweight loss — net social surplus not realised/lost.
Deductible — the part of an insured loss borne by the insured.
Emission cap/quota — a quantitative limit on emissions.
Emission fee — a price/tax per unit of emission.
External benefit/cost — a benefit/damage to third parties not taken into account.
Externality — externality, positive or negative.
Franchise contract — a contract granting use of a business/brand for a payment,
  potentially with strong incentives on outcomes.
Free rider — free rider, benefits without contributing.
Incentive compatibility — the desired choice is preferable to the alternatives.
Individual rationality / participation — the contract is acceptable given the outside option.
Internalization — bringing the external cost/benefit into the private decision.
Limited liability — limited liability, losses/repayments capped.
Lemons market — a market where hidden quality can drive out good quality.
Moral hazard — moral hazard, a private choice/action altered by the contract/incentives.
Open access — open access to a resource, without effective restriction.
Pareto improvement/efficiency — a gain with no loser/no feasible gain without a loser.
Pigouvian tax/subsidy — a levy/aid correcting an externality.
Pooling equilibrium — types not separated by observable behaviour.
Principal-agent problem — a contract with an unobservable action by the agent.
Private benefit — an advantage for the decision-maker, sometimes at the principal's expense.
Private good — rival and excludable.
Procurement — purchase of a service/good by a buyer, often via a reverse auction.
Property rights — rights determining who may use/forbid/trade.
Public good — non-rival and non-excludable, in the pure model.
Reputation — accumulated information on quality/behaviour.
Risk sharing — allocation of exposure to shocks between parties.
Screening — collection/structuring of information by the less informed party.
Separating equilibrium — types revealed by different choices/signals.
Sharecropping — sharing a harvest/revenue between farmer and owner.
Signalling — an observable signal sent by the better-informed party.
Social cost/benefit — total effect, private plus external.
Subsidy — aid lowering the buyer price or raising the seller's revenue.
Tax incidence — the actual distribution of a tax burden.
Tradable permits — permits traded under a total cap.
Tragedy of the commons — overuse due to a scarcity externality.
Trust — trust, reduces some information/contract frictions.

19.6 Games, trade, platforms and auctions
Backward induction — backward induction : start from the last decisions.
Cheap talk — communication that is not directly binding in the game.
Commitment — a commitment credibly altering future choices.
Credible threat — a threat that is optimal to carry out at the node where it is invoked.
Dominant strategy — optimal whatever the others' strategies.
Edgeworth box — a box for the exchange of two goods between two agents.
Free trade — free trade without the barrier considered.
Gains from trade — gains from trade, surplus from mutually useful exchanges.
Multihoming — participation in several platforms.
Network effect — service value depending on participants on the same/other side.
Payoff — the gain/utility of an outcome, distinct from money if preferences are social.
Platform — an intermediary organising interactions between groups.
Pooling/separating — see information; not to be confused with product bundling.
Production possibility frontier — the frontier of feasible combinations.
Pure/mixed strategy — a certain choice/a probabilistic draw between actions.
Reverse auction — a purchasing auction, the low bid can win.
Sealed-bid auction — bids submitted without observing the others.
Sequential/simultaneous moves — decisions taken in order/without observing the rival.
Single-homing — use of a single platform.
Specific factors — factors locked into one sector in the trade model.
Subgame perfect equilibrium — Nash in every subgame, rules out non-credible threats.
Switching costs — costs of changing supplier/platform.
Tariff — a customs duty on imports; different from a two-part tariff.
Terms of trade — export prices relative to import prices.
Vickrey auction — a sealed-bid second-price auction in the private-values model.
Winner's curse — winner's curse due to selecting an optimistic estimate.

19.7 Instruction verbs and words to recognise immediately
Assume — assume : use the given assumption, even if stylised.
Calculate / find / determine — calculate/find : equations, steps, result/unit.
Compare — compare : same benchmark, same assumptions, relevant variables.
Define — define : give a simple meaning, not just a formula.
Derive / show how derived — derive/show : start from the objective and obtain the relation.
Discuss — discuss : mechanism, assumptions and any ambiguity.
Explain why / intuition — explain why : link incentives, the margin and the result.
Illustrate / use a figure — draw with labelled axes, curves, quantities and areas.
Holding constant / given — held given : do not treat this variable as a choice.
In the long run — long run : check entry, exit and the possibility of adjusting inputs.
Maximize / minimize — optimise the objective, not just equalise curves.
Motivate / justify — justify : an economic argument linked to the calculation.
No calculations needed — explain the mechanism; do not replace intuition with numbers.
Per unit — per unit, an amount to multiply by the relevant volume.
Resale impossible — resale impossible : a key condition for segmentation.
Subject to — subject to : write explicitly what limits the choice.
Unilaterally — unilaterally : only the decision-maker changes their strategy.
Welfare maximizing — maximise SOCIAL welfare, distinct from private profit.
