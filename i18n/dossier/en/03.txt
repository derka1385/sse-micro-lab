LECTURES -> SKILLS -> APPLICATIONS
L1, Introduction, Friberg 1.1–2.1 and 19: scarcity, choice, allocation,
  efficiency and distribution, model assumptions, derivatives.
L2, Supply and demand, Friberg 2.2–2.5: direct/inverse functions,
  curve shifts, equilibrium and effects of shocks.
  Basis for A4.2, A6.3 and for policies in every exam.
L3, Consumer choice, Friberg 5.1–5.6: preferences, ordinal utility,
  indifference, MRS, budget, interior or corner choice, behavioural biases.
  A2.2; E2022 Q5; E2021 Q4e; Q5 definitions.
L4, Demand curves and elasticities, Friberg 6.1–6.4: individual demand,
  aggregation, compensation and elasticities. A2.1; E2020 Q5; Lerner.
L5, From technology to costs, Friberg 3.1–3.7: production, MP, isoquants,
  productivity, cost minimisation, cost categories, returns to scale.
  Q5 of E2021, E2023, E2025; very recurrent definitions.
L6, Supply by price taking firms, Friberg 4.1–4.8: MR=MC, p=MC,
  short-run shutdown, long-run exit, aggregation, entry and zero profit.
  E2020 Q5, E2024 Q5; mechanisms of monopolistic competition.
L7, Efficiency, Friberg 7.1–7.5: CS, PS, welfare, DWL, ceiling/floor,
  tax and subsidy. A4.2; policies and graphs in every exam.
L8, Monopoly and monopolistic competition, Friberg 9.1–9.3: MR,
  market power, Lerner, barriers, entry, heterogeneous firms.
  A4.1/A4.3; E2021 Q3; E2022 Q3; E2023 Q1; E2025 Q3d.
L9, Price discrimination, Friberg 12.1: first/third/second degree,
  IC/IR, bundling, subscription and two-part tariff.
  A5.1/A5.2; present in all six past exams.
L10, Oligopoly, Friberg 10.1–10.3: Cournot, Nash, reaction, Stackelberg,
  homogeneous and differentiated Bertrand, number of firms, asymmetric costs.
  A5.3; E2020/21/22/23 Cournot calculations; E2024/25 Bertrand calculations.
L11, Externalities, Friberg 13.1–13.2: private and social costs/benefits,
  Pigou, emissions, permits, Coase, public goods and common resources.
  A6.1/A6.3; big exercises E2020/23/24 and public goods E2025.
L12, Asymmetric information, Friberg 14.1–14.4: adverse selection,
  lemons, signal/screening, moral hazard, principal-agent, risk sharing.
  A6.2; theory or numerical questions in all six exams.
A3: simultaneous/sequential games, social preferences, commitments.
  Extension of the Nash idea mentioned in L10; its cover does not say which
  course it comes from. Do not dismiss it because it is atypical.

FORMAT AND OBSERVED TRENDS
The 2020–2025 exams last 4 hours, have four main questions
totalling 83 points and a Q5 that replaces the midterm, worth 10 points.
The instructions require answers in English, structured and justified.
The 2020–2023 exams state the allowed aids/calculator explicitly; do not
extrapolate a current permission from an old exam.
L1 p.17–18 announces for 2026: exam on 23 October, midterm on 28 September,
midterm covering L1–L6, hand-ins 7 points, midterm 10, final 83, total 100.
Thresholds shown: Pass 50, Good 60, Very good 70, Excellent 80.

Counted by YEAR, not by number of sub-questions:
- Market power and pricing/discrimination: 6/6, 2020–2025.
- Taxes, subsidies or other regulation with surplus reasoning:
  6/6, 2020–2025; includes tariffs and a price ceiling in 2023.
- Asymmetric information: 6/6, 2020–2025.
- Public goods in the strict sense: 5/6, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025.
  2023 deals with a common resource/spectrum; public OR common = 6/6.
- Externalities explicitly treated beyond a simple public-good definition:
  5/6, 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024, 2025.
- International trade or comparative advantage: 5/6,
  2020, 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025.
- Cournot equilibrium calculation: 4/6, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023.
  Qualitative Cournot/Bertrand comparison also in 2025.
- Differentiated Bertrand calculation: 2/6, 2024, 2025.
- Numerical sequential leadership: 2/6, 2021 in quantities, 2024 in prices.
- Explicit auctions: 2/6, 2023 and 2025.
- Platforms/network effects/multihoming as explicit models:
  2/6, 2022 and 2025; other exams use platforms as context.
- Five basic definitions in the optional Q5: 6/6.

HOW A BIG QUESTION IS BUILT
Define a mechanism -> solve a base case -> change one assumption
(tax, entry, costs, discrimination, information or order of moves)
-> compare the equilibria -> draw -> explain the intuition.
Topics combine: monopoly + tax, Cournot + asymmetric costs,
subsidy + externality, platform + discrimination, credit + IC.
