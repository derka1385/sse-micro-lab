11. EXTERNALITIES, POLLUTION, PUBLIC GOODS AND COMMONS — L11
======================================================================

11.1 Private, external, social
Externality: an effect on a third party, not taken into account in the transaction.
Negative: pollution, noise; positive: vaccination, research, network.
MPC / PMC = marginal private cost.
MEC = marginal external cost, the additional DAMAGE.
MSC / SMC = marginal social cost = MPC+MEC.
MPB / PMB = marginal private benefit.
MEB = marginal external benefit, the additional external benefit.
MSB / SMB = marginal social benefit = MPB+MEB.
The notation MC_p and MC_e in E2024 means MPC and MEC respectively.
MEC(Q) is a MARGINAL cost; TOTAL external cost is its integral,
not MEC(Q) itself. If MEC is constant e, total damage=eQ.
If MEC(Q)=Q, total damage=Q^2/2, not Q^2.

Private competitive equilibrium: MPB=MPC.
Social optimum: MSB=MSC.
Negative production externality alone: demand=MPB=MSB and supply=MPC;
the market produces too much. Positive consumption externality alone:
demand=MPB, MSB above it; the market produces too little.
The universal rule is MSB=MSC; the production/consumption distinction
determines which side of the graph to adjust.

11.2 Measuring W and DWL
W(Q)=integral_0^Q [MSB(q)-MSC(q)]dq, minus relevant real fixed costs.
DWL=W(Qeff)-W(Qobserved): area between MSB and MSC over the gap interval.
With a negative externality: W=CS+PS+tax revenue-total external damage.
With a positive one: W=CS+PS-subsidy spending+total external benefit.
The tax/subsidy is a transfer; damages/benefits are real effects
on third parties. The "societal producer surplus" of L11 includes
third-party damages; it differs from the private PS of the firms alone.

L11 p.5–8 and E2023: MPB=100-Q,MPC=10+Q,MEC=Q,MSC=10+2Q.
Private: Q45,p55. Social: Q30,MPB70,MPC40,MEC30.
Unregulated DWL=0.5*(45-30)*45=337.5.
At Q45: MSC100,MSB55; height 45. At the social optimum, a buyer price 70 and a
seller price 40 can be linked by the optimal tax 30.

11.3 Pigou and imperfect intervention
Corrective tax t*=MEC(Qeff), for a negative externality
not already internalised and an otherwise competitive market.
Corrective subsidy s*=MEB(Qeff), the analogous positive case.
Evaluate the marginal value at the EFFICIENT QUANTITY, not automatically
at the initial quantity. If the externality depends on Q, t* is not arbitrary.
A tax can reduce a pre-existing DWL. An excessive subsidy can
overshoot Qeff and recreate a DWL. Always compare with the social optimum, not the private one.

Negative example above, tax 20: 100-Q=10+Q+20 ->Q35,
p_b65,p_s45. Q stays above 30: DWL=0.5*5*15=37.5.
Optimal tax 30 ->Q30,DWL0 in this model.
E2024 textiles: demand200-Q,MPC80+Q,MECQ ->Qpriv60,Qeff40,
DWL0.5*20*60=600; optimal tax 40.
A6 solar: MPB100-3Q,MPC10+2Q,MEB20 ->Qpriv18,p46,
MSB120-3Q ->Qeff22, subsidy 20; uncorrected DWL40.
Buyer price at Q22=34; seller 54; gap 20.

11.4 Pollution and abatement: look at the horizontal axis
MAC=marginal abatement cost, the marginal cost of reducing pollution.
If the axis is ABATEMENT a: MAC often rises as a rises.
If the axis is remaining POLLUTION e: the MAC of the next reduction is
often decreasing in e. The two graphs are compatible.
Optimum: cost of reducing one unit = damage of that unit, MAC=MEC.
Emissions tax: the firm chooses MAC=t; optimal tax=MEC at the optimum.
L11 p.13: on the pollution axis Q, MAC=10-Q, MEC=Q/2.
Without tax Q10; tax 2 ->MAC2 ->Q8, MEC4. This is not the optimum:
optimum Q=20/3≈6.667, tax 10/3≈3.333.
A tax per unit of OUTPUT is equivalent to a tax per unit of EMISSION only if
emission per unit is fixed; otherwise taxing emissions directly can
also encourage cleaner technology.

11.5 Quotas, permits and uncertainty
Quota: fixes a quantity of emissions. Tax: fixes the price of polluting.
Tradable permits: a total cap and transferable rights.
If MAC_L<MAC_H at the initial allocation, L abates more and sells
permits; H buys and abates less. There is a gain as long as MACs differ.
At an interior solution, MAC_L=MAC_H=permit price: the same cap reached at
minimum cost. The initial allocation affects rents, not necessarily
the efficient final allocation under frictionless trading.
An identical quota per firm does not generally minimise total cost.

E2021 Q1d, "prices versus quantities" complement: uncertain abatement
costs and very steep marginal damages ->a quota/cap protects better
against a large error in the quantity of emissions. Relatively flat marginal
damages and steep MAC ->a tax can better avoid an excessive cost
of complying with the quota. An argument about RELATIVE slopes around the optimum,
not a rule "quotas are always better". Draw two possible MACs.

11.6 Coase and property rights
Well-defined rights and costless bargaining ->the parties can negotiate
an efficient quantity, whatever the initial allocation of rights,
under the theorem's assumptions. The allocation changes who pays and incomes.
Intuition: gains from an agreement when reducing the damage is worth more
than the profit it costs; bargain until the gain disappears.
Transaction costs: time, contracts, monitoring, a large number of parties,
free riders, coordination and social norms. They can block
agreements. Uncertain rights ->the theorem's condition is not met.
Bringing polluter and victim under one owner can internalise
the effect, but an owner who becomes a monopolist can introduce another
distortion; do not automatically conclude the socially optimal
competitive quantity. E2024 Q3d and E2025 Q1c.

11.7 Four categories of goods
Excludable: access can be prevented. Rival: one person's use
reduces the possibilities of others.
                       Excludable                 Non-excludable
Rival                  Private good               Common resource
Non-rival              Club good                  Pure public good
Private: sandwich. Club: uncongested subscription/service.
Common: open-access fishery; spectrum with interference (E2023).
Public: defence, disseminated basic research, street cleaning
in the exercise's model. Congestion and access rules can
change the classification. A road/tunnel is not always a pure public
good: tolls are possible and there is rivalry when congested.

11.8 Public good: VERTICAL summation
Everyone benefits from the same Q. MB_social(Q)=sum_i max(0,MB_i(Q)).
Interior optimum: sum MB_i=MC, the Samuelson condition (standard name,
not necessarily used in the slides).
Truncate each negative WTP at 0 if the good is simply not wanted beyond
a quantity; if the good really does harm, the model changes.
Identify the thresholds where each MB reaches zero, then check the segment.
Free rider: benefiting even without paying ->incentive to under-contribute and
to under-reveal one's WTP. Voluntary provision is usually insufficient,
not necessarily zero. Compulsory taxes/norms can help.

L11 p.25: MB_L=10-h, MB_H=20-h.
For h<=10: sum 30-2h. MC26 ->h2; MC14 ->h8.
At MC14, H alone would fund h6, below the collective optimum 8.
At MC26, no individual alone funds anything even though h2 is collectively useful.
E2024 tunnel: MB1=10-Q,MB2=5-Q,MB3=10-2Q,MC9.
On Q<=5, sum 25-4Q; Qeff4, all WTPs positive at 4.
E2025/A6 cleaning: MB15-Q,12-Q,9-Q; sum 36-3Q; MC27 ->Qeff3.
E2022 wifi: 300 people MB10-Q; 300(10-Q)=1500 ->Qeff5.
Do not write Q=300(10-p) for this public good: each person consumes
the SAME speed, not an individual fraction of the speed.

11.9 Common resource and open access
Each user imposes on others a scarcity/congestion they ignore.
Tragedy of the commons: overuse, not a lack of value of the resource.
L11 fishery example: average revenue per boat vs individual cost; entry
until average revenue=cost, whereas the optimum uses the SOCIAL marginal
product of an extra boat. The total catch can deteriorate
with too many boats. Do not use MP and AP as synonyms.
Solutions: property rights, quantity restrictions, permits,
fees, local rules and norms in repeated interactions.
Free rider of the public good = under-contribution; commons = overexploitation.
